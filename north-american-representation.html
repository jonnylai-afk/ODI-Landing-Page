What to have ready

  • Product specifications and full ingredient list
  • Current packaging and label artwork
  • Export price list (EXW or FOB) with MOQ, case pack and pallet configuration
  • Shelf life from production and at shipment

Every engagement is scoped by product, channel and territory, and agreed in writing after an initial assessment. Commercial terms are discussed directly with qualified clients.

Common questions

What does a food broker do in Canada?

A broker represents a manufacturer to buyers: presenting products, negotiating listings, planning promotions and following up on orders, usually without taking ownership of the goods. Through Olympic Distributors, our group can also import and distribute where that suits the product.

Do I need a Canadian importer?

Most food imported into Canada for sale must be imported by a business that holds a Safe Food for Canadians (SFC) licence from the Canadian Food Inspection Agency. You can use an existing licensed importer, or we can discuss acting as importer for you.

Will my labels have to change?

Almost always. Canadian labels need English and French, a Canadian-format nutrition facts table and allergens declared to Canadian rules. Since January 2026, many prepackaged foods high in saturated fat, sugars or sodium also carry the front-of-package nutrition symbol.

Do you cover the United States?

We assess U.S. opportunities case by case, by product and channel, during the initial review.
